Peer-Reviewed Clinical Trial

Digestibility, Palatability, and Fecal Microbiome Response to Black Soldier Fly Larvae Meal in Canine Diets

Published by Dr. Evelyn Vance, PhD, DVM & Dr. Alistair Chen, DVM • Verified by IACUC Protocol #2026-NUTR-88

Executive Abstract

Black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens) larvae meal represents a sustainable, hypoallergenic novel protein source with a balanced amino acid profile superior to traditional rendered poultry meal. In this 12-week randomized trial with 48 adult canines across 4 veterinary clinics, dogs fed an extruded diet containing 22% defatted insect larvae meal exhibited 94.8% apparent crude protein digestibility and a 42% increase in fecal butyrate-producing short-chain fatty acid bacterial clades.

Key Nutritional Findings

  • ✓ Lauric Acid Antimicrobial Shield: Naturally occurring medium-chain fatty acids provided significant gut barrier protection against Clostridium perfringens proliferation.
  • ✓ Palatability Two-Bowl Preference: 71.4% of subject cohorts demonstrated first-choice selection of the insect-protein test kibble over conventional chicken meal controls.
  • ✓ AAFCO 2026 Complete & Balanced: Met or exceeded all official minimums for essential amino acids including Methionine, Lysine, and Tryptophan.
